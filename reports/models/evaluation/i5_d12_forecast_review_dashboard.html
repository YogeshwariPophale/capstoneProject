What the score means

On average, the forecast differs from the observed speed 15 minutes later by about 1.60 mph. This is a speed regression result, not a congestion detection score. June validation MAE: 1.682 mph vs 1.822 for persistence.

The model is HistGradientBoostingRegressor (100 iterations, 31 max leaf nodes); features use recent speed, flow, occupancy, direction, postmile and calendar/time.

Where the model needs attention

Low target speeds (<30 mph): model is worse.
MAE 6.47 mph vs 5.02 mph persistence (20,707 examples). This is the clearest weakness if congestion prediction is the goal.
Rapid changes remain hard.
When speed changes by more than 15 mph, model MAE is 16.89 mph vs 21.10 baseline (17,244 examples). It improves, but errors remain large.
More stable cases are better.
For changes ≤5 mph (1.74M examples), model MAE is 1.22 mph vs 1.25 baseline.

Coverage and interpretation limits

  • District 12 I‑5 mainline only; not statewide or ramps/HOV.
  • September excluded: 85.6% of records fell below the 90% observed threshold.
  • 38 missing source dates in Jan–Sep; only complete five-minute windows included.
  • Speed bins below are descriptive error groups, not approved congestion labels.
  • Large abrupt changes may be real recovery or detector/aggregation anomalies; observed percentage alone cannot distinguish them.
